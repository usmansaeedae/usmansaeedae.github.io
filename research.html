Research overview

Validated models for the hydrogen transition

My research develops trustworthy simulation and machine-learning tools for low-carbon combustion — so that hydrogen engines can be designed, optimised and understood before metal is ever cut.

Durham projects

Theme 01 · Combustion

Hydrogen & low-carbon combustion

The core of my doctoral work: hydrogen–diesel dual-fuel operation of compression-ignition engines, where hydrogen displaces diesel energy while a diesel pilot provides reliable ignition. My studies systematically mapped the levers that matter:

  • Hydrogen energy share — how much diesel can be displaced at high load before performance and emissions trade-offs bite (ICEESEN 2024; IJHE 2025).
  • Injection technique — comparative analysis of hydrogen induction strategies and their effect on performance and emissions (Case Studies in Thermal Engineering 2025).
  • Engine geometry — stroke-to-bore ratio and other geometric variations, and how they shape efficiency and emissions (ECM: X 2026; Energy 2025).

Current work broadens the fuel palette: comparative studies of ammonia, methane, gasoline, hydrogen and diesel in both compression-ignition and spark-ignition engines, presented at ICES 2026 and ICMIAM 2026.

Why it matters

Heavy-duty and off-highway machinery is among the hardest transport to electrify. Hydrogen combustion offers a near-term, carbon-free-at-the-tailpipe pathway that reuses much of today's engine manufacturing base — if the engineering trade-offs are properly understood.

Anchor publication

Hydrogen as a sustainable combustion fuel: performance, challenges, and pathways for transition to low-carbon propulsion systems — Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 2025 (IF 18.0).

Theme 02 · Simulation

High-fidelity engine simulation & validation

I build the full simulation hierarchy: 3D reacting-flow CFD in ANSYS Fluent and Forte with detailed chemistry, 1D system-level models in Ricardo WAVE for whole-engine behaviour, and parametric CAD and meshing (SolidWorks, SpaceClaim, DesignModeler) that let geometry itself become a design variable.

This hierarchy is what makes design-space studies possible: 1D models sweep operating conditions quickly; 3D CFD resolves in-cylinder combustion where the detail matters; and every level is checked against experimental data before its predictions are trusted.

Theme 03 · Machine learning

Physics-informed ML & optimisation

High-fidelity CFD is too expensive to explore thousands of design candidates — so I train surrogate models that learn engine behaviour from simulation data. My work uses two complementary approaches: artificial neural networks and SINDy (sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics), which recovers compact, interpretable equations rather than black-box weights.

These surrogates achieved R² of 0.81–0.99 against CFD while cutting per-point evaluation time from around 87 seconds to a fraction of a microsecond — fast enough to drive NSGA-II multi-objective optimisation with TOPSIS decision-making to select balanced designs from the Pareto front.

Methodology

A validation-first workflow

Every study follows the same disciplined loop — from measured data to optimised design and back to validation.

Impact

Industrial relevance

  • Validated against a JCB test engine — directly relevant to heavy-duty and off-highway machinery, the sectors hardest to electrify.
  • Dual-fuel strategies offer retrofit-friendly pathways that reuse existing engine platforms and manufacturing capability.
  • Surrogate-driven optimisation compresses design iteration from days of CFD to seconds — a workflow industry can adopt.
  • Engagement beyond papers: peer review for Energy and Applied Energy, and co-authored funding bids with industrial consortia (Horizon Europe HORIZON-CL5-2025-02-D2-08; Innovate UK Energy Catalyst Round 11).

Impact

Environmental significance

  • Hydrogen combustion is carbon-free at the point of use — 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O — directly supporting UK net-zero targets.
  • My studies quantify the NOx and efficiency trade-offs that must be managed for hydrogen engines to be genuinely clean.
  • Bioenergy and built-environment reviews extend the contribution to renewable fuel production and low-carbon buildings.

Looking ahead

Future research directions

Two connected directions link hydrogen production and utilisation for low-carbon transport.

Direction 01

Hydrogen production via biomass gasification

Investigating thermochemical conversion pathways to improve hydrogen yield, process efficiency and environmental performance — analysing feedstock characteristics, gasification operating conditions and downstream gas cleaning and separation, with process modelling integrated with lifecycle efficiency evaluation to support renewable hydrogen from waste and biomass resources.

Direction 02

Hydrogen in jet engines & gas turbines

Building on my combustion-modelling expertise to examine combustion stability, ignition characteristics and NOx mitigation using high-fidelity CFD and predictive models — enabling efficient integration of hydrogen into future aircraft propulsion and supporting the UK's sustainable-aviation ambitions.
