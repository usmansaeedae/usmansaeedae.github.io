Postdoctoral research, Durham University

Digital models for cleaner ships

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Digital Twin Lab at Durham University, in Prof. Dawei Wu's research group. My work joins engine physics, machine learning and techno-economics to help shipping and ports cut carbon.

Flagship modelling work

Digital models of a hydrogen–diesel marine engine

The MAN D2862 is a 12-cylinder engine that runs either on diesel alone or on hydrogen and diesel together, the type of engine now used in hydrogen-powered crew transfer vessels.

Measured operating data for engines like this are scarce. So I set out to answer a practical question: how much can a carefully tested machine-learning model tell us when the only data available are the performance curves the manufacturer has published? The aim is to show industry honestly what such a model can do today, and to have it ready for measured engine data when they become available.

The models take engine speed and brake power as inputs and predict diesel consumption in diesel-only mode, and diesel and hydrogen consumption in dual-fuel mode. Efficiency, hydrogen energy share and tank-to-wake CO2 are then calculated from those flows.

D2862 Dual-Fuel Advisor

An interactive web app built on the final models, so engineers can explore the engine without writing code.

  • Operating point: fuel use, efficiency and CO2 at any speed and power, in diesel-only and dual-fuel mode.
  • Compare and what-if: two conditions side by side, or the effect of changing speed or power.
  • Speed choice: which engine speed delivers a required power most efficiently.
  • Reliability label on every result: validated, indicative, extrapolation, or not achievable.

How the models were built

DigitisePerformance curves from the manufacturer's published brochure turned into a structured dataset.
ExtendThe published points expanded to more than 1,000 by extraction along the curves, with extracted and interpolated values kept separate.
CompareSeven model families screened, with errors reported in units and as a share of each output's range.
SelectTOPSIS multi-criteria ranking, then support vector regression and neural networks compared head to head.
Test and deployAccuracy checked on a hidden block of engine speeds the models never saw, then packaged in the advisor app.

What the work shows

  • Published manufacturer data can support useful first-pass models of a dual-fuel marine engine.
  • Honest testing matters: errors are reported on speeds held back from training, not only on the training range.
  • Every number in the app traces back to code and output tables.

Honest limits

  • Built from published brochure data, not test-bed measurements.
  • No NOx, soot, exhaust temperature or cylinder pressure data, so these are not predicted.
  • Hydrogen consumption is the hardest output to predict. CO2 is tank-to-wake only.
  • Next steps: validation against measured engine data and wider operating coverage. A journal paper is in preparation.

Funded projects

Where my postdoc work sits

SEASTARS

Horizon Europe Innovation Action (grant 101192901). Led by the University of Strathclyde, with Durham University as a research partner.

seastars-project.eu

The project

SEASTARS (Sustainable Emission Abatement Strategies and Technologies for Advanced Revolutionary Ships) aims to show at least a 30% cut in well-to-wake greenhouse gas emissions by 2030, compared with 2008, and a 20% gain in energy efficiency, compared with 2022, across eight market-ready vessel designs: four retrofits and four new builds for inland, short-sea and deep-sea shipping. It combines hull and propeller optimisation, air lubrication, fuel cells, electric drives, solar power and alternative fuels such as biofuels, hydrogen and ammonia, using model-based systems engineering.

My part

My engine modelling, including the D2862 digital models above, is part of Durham's contribution. I am also helping to prepare the project's fourth General Assembly, which Durham hosts on 21 and 22 January 2027.

Karachi Port decarbonisation

Funded by GIZ. Partners: Durham University, University of Birmingham and the University of Management and Technology (UMT), Lahore.

The project

Decarbonising Karachi Port Operations and the Karachi–Pipri Dedicated Freight Corridor. Durham works on the port side, focusing on the port's own service fleet, such as tugs, pilot boats and mooring boats. The University of Birmingham covers the freight rail corridor.

My part

I review the evidence on port operations and emissions, map the project's stakeholders and present progress to the partners. The next project session is at UMT Lahore in February 2027.

HI-ACT hub

EPSRC hub: Hydrogen Integration for Accelerated Energy Transitions. Prof. Dawei Wu is part of the hub team.

hi-act.ac.uk

The project

HI-ACT is a national research hub studying how hydrogen and alternative liquid fuels can fit into the UK's whole energy system, weighing the technical, economic, social and policy questions together.

My part

Within our group I contribute to the hub's hydrogen research, including techno-economic analysis and open-access modelling tools.
