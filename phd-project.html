Doctoral research · 2023–2026

Assessment of hydrogen–diesel dual-fuelled compression-ignition engines

University of Huddersfield · Vice-Chancellor's PhD Scholarship · PhD awarded August 2026, after a viva passed with minor corrections in April 2026.

3D CFD1D simulationExperimental validationSINDy & ANNNSGA-II + TOPSIS

§ 01 · The problem

Heavy-duty transport is hard to decarbonise

Trucks, construction plant and off-highway machinery demand high energy density, long duty cycles and fast refuelling — conditions where batteries struggle. Compression-ignition engines dominate these sectors, so a credible low-carbon pathway for the CI engine itself has outsized climate value. Hydrogen, burning to water with no carbon at the point of use, is one of the most promising candidates.

§ 02 · The gap

Promising fuel, incomplete map

Hydrogen–diesel dual-fuel operation — hydrogen displacing diesel energy while a diesel pilot ignites the charge — had shown promise, but the interacting effects of hydrogen energy share, injection technique and engine geometry had not been systematically mapped for modern heavy-duty engines. And the tool best suited to studying them, high-fidelity CFD, is far too expensive to explore a broad design space, let alone optimise across it.

§ 03 · Objectives

What the project set out to do

  • Build experimentally validated 1D and 3D models of hydrogen–diesel dual-fuel combustion.
  • Quantify how hydrogen energy share, injection technique and geometric parameters (including stroke-to-bore ratio) shape performance and emissions.
  • Develop machine-learning surrogate models fast enough to make design-space exploration practical.
  • Deliver a multi-objective optimisation framework (NSGA-II with TOPSIS decision-making) for balanced engine design.

Project at a glance

§ 04 · Methodology

A hierarchy of models, anchored to data

No single tool answers every question, so the project built a chain of them — each validated, each feeding the next.

Experimental foundation

Measurements from a JCB test engine provided the ground truth against which every model was checked, keeping predictions honest from the start.

1D system simulation

Ricardo WAVE models captured whole-engine behaviour across operating conditions — extended with a custom C++ fuel-property module I developed to represent hydrogen–diesel blends accurately.

3D reacting-flow CFD

ANSYS Fluent and Forte with detailed chemistry resolved in-cylinder mixing, ignition and combustion, with parametric geometry and meshes built in SolidWorks, SpaceClaim and DesignModeler.

Machine-learning surrogates

SINDy and artificial neural networks learned the simulated response, turning hours of CFD into microsecond predictions suitable for optimisation.

§ 05 · Workflow

From measurement to optimised design

1D modelWAVE + custom C++ module sweeps the operating space.
3D CFDFluent / Forte with chemistry resolves the combustion detail.
SurrogateSINDy / ANN learn the response surface.
OptimiseNSGA-II maps trade-offs; TOPSIS picks balanced designs.
ValidateOptima re-checked against high-fidelity models and data.

§ 06 · Validation

Earning trust in the models

Both the 1D WAVE models and the 3D CFD were validated against measurements from the JCB test engine before being used predictively. This validation-first discipline — agree with the data you have before extrapolating to conditions you don't — runs through every study in the thesis and every paper that came from it.

Detailed validation plots and quantitative comparisons are presented in the thesis and associated publications.

§ 07 · Results

What the project established

  • Systematically mapped how hydrogen energy share, injection technique and geometry (including stroke-to-bore ratio) shape efficiency and emissions trade-offs — published across IJHE, Energy, ECM: X and CSTE.
  • Surrogate models achieved R² of 0.81–0.99 against CFD while cutting per-point evaluation time from ~87 s to ~4.5×10−7 s.
  • The SINDy + NSGA-II + TOPSIS framework made multi-objective engine optimisation computationally practical — published in IJHE, 2026.

§ 08 · Challenges

The hard parts

Cost versus coverage. Detailed chemistry makes each CFD run expensive; design studies need thousands of evaluations. The resolution was architectural: use fidelity to teach cheap models, then let the cheap models roam.

Hydrogen's character. Wide flammability limits and low ignition energy make hydrogen combustion behaviour sensitive and demanding to capture — and make careful validation non-negotiable.

Tooling gaps. Standard 1D fuel libraries did not represent hydrogen–diesel blends the way the work required — which is why the custom C++ fuel-property module for WAVE had to be written.

§ 09 · Lessons learned

What the PhD taught me

  • Validate first; predict second. Credibility is built in that order.
  • Automate relentlessly — scripted, repeatable workflows turn one study into a family of studies.
  • Interpretability matters: an equation you can read (SINDy) earns more trust than a black box with the same R².
  • Publish as you go — peer review sharpened the work at every stage.

§ 10 · Industrial applications

Where this lands in practice

Validation against a JCB engine ties the work directly to heavy-duty and off-highway machinery. Dual-fuel operation offers a retrofit-friendly route that preserves existing engine platforms, and the surrogate-driven optimisation workflow transfers to any simulation-heavy design task — from engines to gas turbines. The same modelling philosophy also underpins funding bids I co-authored with industrial consortia for Horizon Europe and Innovate UK.

§ 11 · Environmental benefits

Combustion without carbon

Hydrogen combustion releases no CO2 at the point of use. The project's contribution is to make that promise engineerable: quantifying the efficiency and NOx trade-offs, and providing the optimisation tools to manage them — supporting the UK's net-zero targets in the sectors where electrification is least tractable.

§ 12 · Future work

Where the research goes next

Multi-fuel combustion

Already under way: comparative ammonia, methane, gasoline and hydrogen studies in CI and SI engines, presented at ICES 2026 and ICMIAM 2026. My postdoctoral work at Durham University now carries the modelling into marine engines.

Renewable hydrogen production

Biomass gasification pathways — feedstocks, operating conditions, gas cleaning and separation — modelled and optimised for yield and lifecycle efficiency.

Hydrogen aviation

Combustion stability, ignition and NOx mitigation for hydrogen in jet engines and gas turbines, using high-fidelity CFD and predictive models.
